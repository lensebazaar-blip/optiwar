PD Measurement Guide

How to measure your Pupillary Distance for accurate prescription glasses

What is PD?

Pupillary Distance (PD) is the distance in millimetres between the centres of your two pupils. It determines where the optical centres of your lenses are positioned — get it right and your vision is sharp and comfortable. Get it wrong and you may experience eye strain, headaches, or blurred vision.

Typical adult PD ranges from 54mm to 74mm (average: 63mm women, 65mm men). Children's PD ranges from 43mm to 58mm.

Method 2: Mirror + Ruler

  1. Stand 20cm (8 inches) from a mirror. Look straight ahead.
  2. Hold a millimetre ruler flat against your brow, resting on the bridge of your nose.
  3. Close your right eye. Align the 0mm mark with the centre of your left pupil.
  4. Open your right eye, close your left. Read the mm mark at the centre of your right pupil.
  5. Repeat 3 times and take the average.

Single PD vs Dual PD

Type Example When to use
Single PD 63mm Total distance between both pupils. Most common format on prescriptions.
Dual PD R: 31.5 / L: 32.0 Per-eye measurement from nose centre. More precise — ideal for high prescriptions.

Either format works for ordering at Optiwar. Optiwar's face measurement tool provides dual PD automatically.

Near PD vs Distance PD

Distance PD — measured when looking at far objects. Used for most single-vision glasses.

Near PD — measured when looking at close objects (reading). Typically 3-4mm less than distance PD because your eyes converge inward.

  • Single-vision distance glasses → use distance PD
  • Dedicated reading glasses → use near PD
  • Progressive / bifocal lenses → both may be used (our team handles this)

If your prescription lists only one PD, it's your distance PD — that's what Optiwar uses by default.
